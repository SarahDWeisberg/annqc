AnnQC Cross-Tool Benchmark

Comparing QC outcomes across 7 public scRNA-seq datasets using four methods.
Published: thresholds from original paper or tutorial  ·  AnnQC manual: annqc with published thresholds  ·  AnnQC auto: --auto-thresholds, no prior knowledge  ·  Seurat defaults: min.features=200, percent.mt<5% (tissue-agnostic)  ·  scater 3MAD: isOutlier(log=TRUE, nmads=3) on genes/counts, isOutlier on mito

Seurat defaults: Applied exactly as in the Seurat v5 PBMC tutorial — min.features=200, percent.mt<5%. No max_genes or min_counts unless specified in the original published threshold. This represents the "out-of-the-box" Seurat workflow applied to every dataset identically, regardless of tissue type.

scater isOutlier (3 MAD): Applied as in OSCA best practices — isOutlier(log=TRUE, type="both", nmads=3) for genes and total counts, isOutlier(type="higher", nmads=3) for mito percentage. This is fully data-adaptive: thresholds are derived from each dataset's own distribution.

Key Findings

  • Seurat defaults (5% mito) perform well on PBMC datasets but catastrophically over-filter non-blood tissues (kidney, intestine, thymus). Kidney: removes ~55% of cells.
  • scater isOutlier (3 MAD) is tissue-adaptive and produces reasonable results across most datasets, but the log1p MAD can over-retain damaged cells in datasets with skewed distributions (e.g. bone marrow).
  • AnnQC manual matches published outcomes most closely across all 7 datasets (configures tissue-appropriate thresholds explicitly).
  • AnnQC auto (MAD-based, no prior knowledge) derives tissue-specific thresholds automatically, outperforming Seurat defaults on every non-PBMC dataset.
  • Reference atlas comparison adds a new capability not present in Seurat or scater: flagging datasets whose QC distributions are statistically anomalous for the claimed tissue type.
